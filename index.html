What is an oral probiotic?

Feeding the mouth's own ecosystem

The oral microbiome is the community of microorganisms that live in the mouth. Its composition varies across people and mouth sites and is influenced by factors including diet and oral hygiene. It is an active area of research, but a simple “good versus bad bacteria” description does not capture that complexity.

An oral probiotic is a product marketed to provide live microorganisms in the mouth. Findings from a particular strain, dose, delivery method or study population cannot be assumed to apply to other products. The current ProDentim label and complete strain identities were not available in the seller FAQ reviewed here.

Oral probiotics are not a replacement for established oral-care practices. Brushing with fluoride toothpaste, cleaning between teeth and professional dental care have distinct roles. Mouthwash ingredients and effects also vary, so a single broad comparison should not be taken as applying to every product.

Scientific research & oral health references

Selected research on oral probiotics

These publications study specific probiotic strains, products, doses and outcomes. The seller FAQ reviewed here does not identify ProDentim's strains or provide its complete current formula, so we cannot match these studies to the product. None of the references below is a clinical trial of the finished ProDentim formula. Findings vary, and category-level research does not establish that this product works.

  • 1

    Clinical and microbiological effects of Lactobacillus reuteri probiotics in chronic periodontitis

    Teughels W, et al. — Journal of Clinical Periodontology — 2013

    A randomized, placebo-controlled trial finding that L. reuteri lozenges, used alongside standard scaling and root planing, produced greater improvements in pocket depth and attachment level than cleaning alone.

    Read research
  • 2

    Lactobacillus reuteri-containing tablets and gingivitis — randomized clinical trial

    Iniesta M, et al. — Journal of Clinical Periodontology — 2012

    An 8-week placebo-controlled study finding L. reuteri tablets reduced levels of selected periodontal pathogens in the subgingival microbiota, without a matching clinical improvement in gingivitis symptoms.

    Read research
  • 3

    Effect of probiotic Streptococcus salivarius K12 on oral malodour parameters

    Burton JP, et al. — Journal of Applied Microbiology — 2006

    Found that bacteriocin-producing S. salivarius K12, given after an antimicrobial mouthwash, reduced volatile sulfur compound levels associated with bad breath.

    Read research
  • 4

    Clinical efficacy of probiotics on oral health: a systematic review of clinical trials

    Navidifar T, et al. — Current Pharmaceutical Biotechnology — 2023

    A systematic review of clinical trials on oral probiotics. Its conclusions concern varied interventions and do not establish the safety or efficacy of ProDentim.

    Read research
  • 5

    Probiotics for managing caries and periodontitis: systematic review and meta-analysis

    Gruner D, Paris S, Schwendicke F — Journal of Dentistry — 2016

    Reported that evidence was insufficient to recommend probiotics for caries; evidence for gingivitis and periodontitis was more supportive but limited by study quality and available data.

    Read research
  • 6

    The effect of probiotics on halitosis: a systematic review and meta-analysis

    Yoo JI, et al. — Probiotics and Antimicrobial Proteins — 2019

    Found probiotics produced a moderate improvement in organoleptic (smell-based) halitosis scores, though not a significant reduction in measured volatile sulfur compounds.

    Read research
  • 7

    The effect of Streptococcus salivarius K12 on halitosis — double-blind, randomized, placebo-controlled trial

    He L, et al. — Probiotics and Antimicrobial Proteins — 2020

    Found S. salivarius K12 alone did not significantly reduce halitosis from tongue coating unless the coating was first physically or chemically cleaned.

    Read research
  • 8

    Role of probiotics in halitosis of oral origin: systematic review and meta-analysis

    Lopez-Valverde N, et al. — Frontiers in Nutrition — 2021

    A meta-analysis of randomized clinical studies concluding that some probiotic strains have a beneficial effect on halitosis, while noting more high-quality trials are needed.

    Read research
